How we work

The agenda is the product.

Anyone can book a ballroom. What is difficult — and what takes two decades to build — is knowing which principal secretary is three months from a procurement decision, and which vendor has actually shipped the thing they are about to present.

Our programme committees are drawn from the editorial desks, not from sales. A session earns its slot because it answers a question the sector is currently stuck on. Sponsors get a speaking slot on the record; they do not get to write the agenda.

Government officers attend free at every Elets event. That is not generosity — it is the reason the rooms are worth attending. It also means the delegate mix skews toward people who sign off on things rather than people selling to them.

Milestones

How it grew

Founded by Dr Ravi Gupta, who remains CEO and Editor-in-Chief. The starting proposition: cover technology in the public sector properly, because nobody else was.

Three titles covering e-governance, education technology and health systems. Between them they still reach several million readers a month.

The World Education Summit, Healthcare Innovation Summit and NBFC100 Tech Summit become annual fixtures — now in their 36th, 19th and 23rd editions respectively.

Completing coverage across the four sectors where India's public and private institutions meet most often.

A second office in Dubai, plus international editions of the education and health summits. Session video crosses ten million views.

Held 22 January at Eros Hotel, Nehru Place, New Delhi. 350 delegates, 60 speakers, 20 partners — and the AI programme now runs across every sector we cover.
